# Methods: ways of running a session

A method is a session shape, built out of the moves in SKILL.md section 2. These are exemplars, not a menu: they show what a well-chosen shape looks like so you can adapt one, combine two, or invent another for the learner in front of you. The cases are real. Read this when choosing or switching a method, not every turn.

Choosing: go by phase, mode (voice, text, phone, board), the learner's energy and the time left. A learner who can judge gets a short informed choice ("the walk-through suits tonight; whole questions are better if this topic is new"); one who can't gets your pick, watched. Any shape can be dropped mid-session.

## Whole questions, working photographed

- **Looks like:** real questions (or ones written to a dot point in the style of recent papers), smallest first. The learner works on paper and sends a photo; feedback goes on the actual working.
- **Fits:** skill topics (calculations, show-thats, extended responses), exam technique, the last week. It finds execution slips and missing final parts that only show in real working.
- **Doesn't fit:** a concept not met yet (teach it first, or use a smaller whole question); voice only; no pen and camera; broad sweeps, since it's slow per question.
- **Failing when:** most questions need help (they're too big, or the concept isn't there), or one question has eaten the session.
- **Case:** the default through two maths and science exam blocks. A photographed solution showed a correct method that stopped one line short of what the question asked, which a typed final answer would have hidden.

## Formula walk-through

- **Looks like:** one topic at a time, each formula exactly as printed in the official formula book: every symbol and its unit, when it applies and when it doesn't, one concrete scenario with numbers, the exam wording that triggers it, the usual trap. Learner-led (they photograph one formula; you reply with scenario, trigger, trap and one try-this), which suits text in a quiet place; or teacher-led (a whole section as a table on the board), which suits voice.
- **Fits:** the last days before a formula-heavy exam, once topics have been met; building a feel for which formula a question wants; finding topics never seen; low energy.
- **Doesn't fit:** as a stand-in for solo whole questions. It's recognition, not production, so a skill stays at With help. Learning a concept from zero (give the why first). Subjects with no formula book.
- **Failing when:** the try-this questions come back wrong (the topic needs teaching, not a walk-through), or the learner is nodding along without producing anything.
- **Case:** two days before a physics exam, the learner's own idea. It drew out their "why" questions (whose frame the muon's lifetime is measured in), caught a misremembered P = IR², and surfaced a notation question (two different q's in one line of the formula book) worth answering. Next day, learner-led through one section with a try-this each: 5 of 5 right first time. They skipped formulas they said they knew; that was fine.

## Voice conversation

- **Looks like:** short spoken turns, one small question at a time; the board holds anything to look at.
- **Fits:** qualitative concepts, why chains, misconceptions, definitions, comparing models; quick recall rounds; diagnostic sweeps.
- **Doesn't fit:** long calculations or written answers; anything needing exact symbols read aloud; a quiet place; a phone away from the board; research.
- **Failing when:** the learner asks you to repeat things, loses numbers, or goes quiet after a long turn.
- **Case:** feedback on one answer plus a new numeric question in a single spoken turn "went over my head", and calculations in voice with no formula book in view felt like the wrong use of the session. One thing per turn, and the calculations moved to text.

## Cold diagnostic sweep

- **Looks like:** small whole questions across a list of topics. Leave a topic as soon as one comes back clean; dig in only where one doesn't.
- **Fits:** content met before, in class or in an overlapping subject; the first session of a subject; re-planning when time is short.
- **Doesn't fit:** new content; a learner who is shaken by a run of misses close to an exam (start with ones they'll get).
- **Failing when:** nearly everything misses. It has turned into teaching: stop sweeping and plan properly.
- **Case:** a plan gave a whole day to calculus in a second maths subject, every skill marked Not started, a week after the learner had sat a harder maths exam covering most of it. A short cold check replaced the day, and the time went to the content only the second subject has.

## Morning cold recap

- **Looks like:** whatever was new in an evening session gets a short cold recap first thing the next morning, before anything else.
- **Fits:** learners who study at night; new terms and definitions; any time there is a morning before the exam.
- **Doesn't fit:** when there is no next morning, or the evening session was all practice of known skills.
- **Failing when:** the recap turns into re-teaching most of it. The evening session went too fast or too late.
- **Case:** asked for by a learner who said sleep is when things settle for them. The morning recap after a night of new particle-physics terms showed which had stuck (the ones they'd said back in a sentence) and which hadn't (the ones only heard).

## Hard unfamiliar questions, final days

- **Looks like:** in the last days, mostly complex unfamiliar questions written in the style of the recent papers' hardest ones (research those first). Topics still uncovered are learned through the questions. On a stall, teach the background skill the question rests on, not the trick for that question.
- **Fits:** a learner whose basics are mostly in place and whose marks will come from the hard end; the last few days.
- **Doesn't fit:** shaky basics (each question becomes a lecture); a learner whose confidence a wall of hard questions would damage.
- **Failing when:** every stall traces to a different missing basic. Go back to a sweep.
- **Case:** a researched question in the style of a paper's hardest type paid off directly in the exam a few days later.

## Between-papers sprint

- **Looks like:** on exam day, between two papers: straight into second-paper-style questions on known weak spots. No new content, no long explanations.
- **Fits:** an hour or two between papers; a learner who wants to stay warm.
- **Doesn't fit:** a learner who needs the break, which is often the better call. Ask.
- **Failing when:** a miss needs real teaching. Give the answer and the one rule, and move on.

## Taxonomy first

- **Looks like:** for a new family of named categories, the whole tree once: the top split, then the sub-splits, one example each, with word roots (bary = heavy, meso = middle). Then add terms a level at a time.
- **Fits:** any topic that is mostly names and how they sort: particle families, functional groups, literary techniques, parts of speech, anatomy.
- **Doesn't fit:** two or three terms (just teach them); categories the learner already has.
- **Failing when:** they can recite the tree but can't sort a fresh example. Switch to sorting examples.
- **Case:** four or five new names in voice with no structure got "far out… so many words". The tree plus word roots fixed the sorting at once: 3 of 3 on fresh ones.

## Adding to this file

A shape belongs here once it has worked across several sessions, ideally for more than one subject or learner. Until then it lives in that learner's Method toolbox tab. Write it in the shape above, with the learner's specifics taken out and one real case kept.
